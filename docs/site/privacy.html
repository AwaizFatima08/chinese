Privacy Policy

HomiLabs Tools · Last updated 17 October 2026

Our tools are built to work without accounts and, wherever possible, without collecting anything. Each app has its own section below that says exactly what it uses. If an app's section says it collects nothing, it collects nothing.

Apps covered by this policy
  • Beam: Torch, Strobe & SOS
  • System Pulse: Net Speed Test
  • Business Calculator
  • Lingo15: Chinese & German

1. Who we are

The apps listed on tools.homilabs.org ("the tools") are made by HomiLabs ("we", "us"). This policy covers the tools and this website. Contact: homilabs.smc@gmail.com.

2. Our approach

  • No accounts. None of the tools asks you to sign up or log in.
  • On-device first. Settings and preferences are stored only on your device and are removed when you uninstall the app.
  • Least permissions. A tool only asks for a permission when a feature cannot work without it, and its section below explains why.
  • No selling of data. We never sell personal information.

3. Advertising

Some tools may be supported by advertising. A tool shows ads only if its section below says so, and that section then names the ad provider, what the provider collects (for example the device's advertising ID, approximate location derived from the IP address, and how you interact with ads) and why. Where the law requires it, you will be asked for consent before personalised ads are shown, and you can change that choice in the app.

On Android you can reset or delete your advertising ID at any time in Settings → Privacy → Ads (or Settings → Google → Ads on some phones).

4. This website

tools.homilabs.org uses no cookies, analytics or trackers. Like any website, our hosting provider may keep standard server logs (such as IP address, browser type and the pages requested) for security and to keep the site running. We do not use these logs to identify visitors.

5. Children

The tools are not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from children. If you believe a child has provided us information, contact us and we will delete it.

6. Your rights

Because the tools do not collect personal information unless their section says otherwise, there is usually nothing for us to access, correct or delete. Where a tool does involve a third party (such as an ad provider), its section explains how to exercise your choices. You can always contact us with any privacy question or request, and we will reply within 30 days.

7. Changes

We update this policy when a tool changes what it uses, before that version is released. The "last updated" date above and the date in each app's section show when they last changed.

System Pulse: Net Speed Test

Android · package com.systempulse.app · Section updated 8 October 2026 (version 1.0.1)

System Pulse measures your internet connection and works out whether a slowdown is caused by your Wi-Fi or by your internet provider. It has no account and no sign-up. It shows ads and can use your approximate location; both are explained below.

What System Pulse usesWhyLeaves your phone?
Speed test results (download and upload speed, ping, jitter, loaded latency, quality scores) To show your results and keep a history in the app. Stored on your phone. May be sent, anonymised, to our coverage database (see below).
Approximate location (optional) To tag each test with the area it was run in, so the coverage map can show speeds by area. You can deny the permission and the app still works; tests are then saved without a location. Only after rounding to roughly a 500 m grid. Your exact position is never stored or sent.
Public IP address and internet provider name (looked up through ipwho.is) To show which provider the test was run on. The provider name may be sent, anonymised, to our coverage database. The IP address is removed first.
Wi-Fi details (network name, router address, signal strength, link speed) To work out whether your Wi-Fi is the bottleneck. No. The network name and router address are removed before anything is sent.
Advertising ID and ad-related device data To show ads through Google AdMob, which keep the app free. Yes, to Google. See Advertising below.

Coverage map

To build a map of connection quality by area, the app may upload anonymised test results (speed, latency, provider name and the rounded ~500 m location) to a database run by HomiLabs on Google Firebase. Uploaded results contain no IP address, Wi-Fi network name, router address or any identifier linked to you or your device, so they cannot be traced back to you and cannot be individually deleted.

Permissions

  • Internet / network state / Wi-Fi state – to run the test and read Wi-Fi signal details.
  • Location (approximate and precise, optional) – only to tag tests with a rounded location. The app asks on first launch; you can deny it or revoke it later in Android settings.

Services the app connects to

  • Cloudflare (speed.cloudflare.com, 1.1.1.1) – sends and receives test data to measure speed and latency. Cloudflare sees your IP address, as any website would. Cloudflare privacy policy
  • ipwho.is – returns your provider's name from your IP address. ipwho.is privacy policy
  • Google Firebase – hosts the anonymised coverage database. Firebase privacy

Advertising

System Pulse shows ads through Google AdMob. Google may collect the device's advertising ID, approximate location derived from the IP address, and how you interact with ads, and may use these to personalise ads. See how Google uses data from apps that use its services. You can reset or delete your advertising ID in Settings → Privacy → Ads (or Settings → Google → Ads).

Deleting your data

Your test history is stored only on your phone and is deleted when you uninstall the app.
